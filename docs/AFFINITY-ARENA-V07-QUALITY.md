## Why this replaces 0.5/0.6

The user entered the existing public World on a Samsung Galaxy S24 FE (SM-S721U1) and reported unreadably small buttons, no clear continuation after selecting a team, and a forest environment inconsistent with a music game. This is real user feedback, not a screenshot or a passed physical-device acceptance test.

The revised game replaces the card/HP duel with a musical performance competition. Existing legacy files and tests remain for regression/history; `src/index.ts` now selects `concert-scene.tsx`.

## Implemented

- Enclosed 32×32 club spanning four parcels, landscape terrain disabled, dark architecture and vibrant crew-colored stage fixtures, trusses, speakers, six named studios and distinct instruments.
- Original generated 3×2 emblem atlas for K-pop, Brazilian Funk, Latin Urban, Afrobeats, Hip-hop and Electronic. Emblems appear in the crew selector, studios and NPC winner placards. It is illustrative art, not a gameplay screenshot.
- Density-aware UI scaling with explicit enlarged mode and recovery; a paginated crew selector on short landscape screens, a dedicated three-lane instrument and 64-logical-pixel note pads. Physical readability remains to be tested.
- Selection enters a 20-second matchmaking queue and immediate local soundcheck. Joining auto-readies the real participant; cancellation is available. Four real participants start together early; otherwise labeled bots fill only empty seats after the countdown. No bots are counted as real visitors.
- Six seven-second scored rehearsal rounds, followed by five twelve-second main-stage rounds. Notes and original 80–130 BPM audio reuse the established timing charts. The main-stage five-second lead-in provides a transition/countdown; sound is opt-in.
- Each round measures rhythm (successful notes), precision (hits within 120 ms), harmony (both teammates hit corresponding notes) and consistency (longest streak per participant within that round). Scores normalize to 0–100 and average over each phase.
- Each final attribute is `(studio + 2 × live) / 3`, rounded to two decimals. The sum of four final attributes (maximum 400) decides the winner. A tie is a draw; real-player departure cancels the show. The round resets after 24 seconds.
- NPC audience members raise the winning crew's illustrated placards, or celebrate both crews on a draw. This visualizes the score; it is not voting by real spectators. Reduced motion keeps static celebration signs and fixed equalizer bars.
- Scene-local travel is requested when entering a studio and the main stage. If the client rejects it, copy tells players to follow the signs. No wallet transaction, public network port or new backend is needed.
